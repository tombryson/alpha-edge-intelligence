## What remains wrong

### Financial mechanics still fail in the final text

The short-government investment case says a fast pivot with bull steepening produces mark-to-market losses in the 1-3 year bucket. That does not follow: falling short yields raise existing fixed-rate short-bond prices. Relative performance against long bonds, price effects and reinvestment risk are different questions. See the [SEC's interest-rate-risk explanation](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-86).

The same section says cash at 11% is larger than the 14% short-government sleeve. This is an unambiguous internal numerical contradiction.

The TIPS downside says falling core inflation and breakevens below 2% make TIPS lose on both real-yield and breakeven legs. A fall in breakevens does not establish a rise in real yields or a negative absolute TIPS return. Real yields may fall too. Relative performance against a nominal bond is not the same as an absolute loss. The [Federal Reserve's same-maturity nominal/real yield decomposition](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/tips-yield-curve-and-inflation-compensation.htm) and [Treasury's CPI-indexation mechanics](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/tips/) are the relevant distinctions.

The play review calls the spread over the current policy rate a term premium; another investment case similarly labels a two-year yield versus a current cash proxy. That spread also embeds expected future short rates. It is not an identified term-premium estimate. The [New York Fed explains the decomposition](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2023/per231116).

Other statements remain too strong: bullion supposedly delivers the same gold sensitivity as miners, gold categorically fails in deflation, and an unspecified pharma/biotech sleeve is treated as defensive cash flow. These need conditional instrument- and holding-level reasoning, not labels. No individual positions were supplied in this run.

### Calculation validation is too literal

All four requested calculations are unresolved. The clearest false rejection is the 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread: the saved facts contain 5.01% and 4.76%, from the same source, date and vintage, but the checker rejects them because their metric strings differ. The arithmetic is 0.25 percentage points, or 25bp; that is a yield-curve spread, not a term-premium estimate.

`backend/portfolio_judgment.py:calculate_financial_claims` requires identical metric strings before considering `difference`. `measurement_issues` also requires matching observation dates/reference periods, preventing an explicit change-over-time calculation from being represented. The system needs operation-specific compatibility for curve spreads, time changes and index comparisons. Removing all validation would recreate the original mismatched-horizon problem; adding more prose warnings does not solve it either.

### Review closure is not final-document consistency

Seven corrections are recorded, six finding checks say resolved, and a scoped reconsideration completed. Yet stronger, stale variants survive elsewhere: cash's real stance still explicitly pays cash in an alternative, bullion still has the same sensitivity as miners, and the erroneous bond/TIPS downside text remains. Correcting a quoted field and confirming its replacement is present cannot establish semantic consistency across the whole memo.

The final reconsideration changes wording but no target weights. That can be legitimate; a concentration reduction need not depend on a price forecast. But the run does not demonstrate that unchanged 14% short-government, 8% TIPS and 5% managed-futures weights are now quantitatively justified. The lower-turnover alternative remains unfunded because 15 residual sleeves have no structured final weights, despite prose saying to hold everything else unchanged. No quantified hold-versus-change scenario comparison is available.

### Research effort is not well aimed

All six supplementary Parallel requests returned HTTP 402 insufficient credit. Their receipts retain errors and `completion_mode=with_limitations`, but the progress counter says six completed. Completed processing must not be confused with successful retrieval.

Even with credit, the selected plan is inefficient: three of six questions overlap on uranium balances, contract prices and restart economics. None targets fertiliser. Several questions ask what will happen to inflation or oil rather than requesting the specific observable inputs that would help assess those possibilities. The latest packet also loses direct TIPS real-yield observations that were available in the previous run.

The correct graceful-degradation behavior preserved the memo. It did not complete its research or justify every affected recommendation.

## Highest-value next work

1. **Publish from one authoritative decision record.** Keep competing proposals and detailed reviews inspectable, but make final rationale, numbers and implementation consistent everywhere. Include financial direction/sign checks and scalar comparisons, not only exact-text replacement checks.
2. **Fix calculation semantics.** Define curve-spread and time-change operations with explicit units, instruments and periods. Preserve valid analysis; isolate unsupported assumptions instead of refusing legitimate arithmetic.
3. **Make research decision-specific.** Deduplicate overlapping questions, cover each material play leg, reuse matching available observations, and request missing prices, margins, inventories or term data rather than asking retrieval to settle future outcomes. Distinguish failed retrieval from completed processing.
4. **Separate merit, fit and action.** A sound thesis can still be oversized. An unresearched idea is not disproved. Avoid/underweight is not short. Conditional adds need to remain visibly conditional in the recommendation.
5. **Finish the implementation comparison.** Carry explicit unchanged weights into the small-change plan, then compare funded alternatives without invented costs or expected returns. Do not turn every retained placeholder into another high-conviction allocation.
6. **Restore the concise legacy reading order.** Executive conclusion, current/ideal/proposed allocation, consequential differences, play verdicts, implementation and monitoring; detailed research and audit material afterwards. Consolidate repetition rather than truncating provider outputs or imposing another arbitrary token ceiling.

These are focused corrections to the existing process, not a case for adding more review agents. Keep the independent analysis, source links and graceful recovery. The next improvement should be fewer contradictions and a better finished argument, not more completed stages.
